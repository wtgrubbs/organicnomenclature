IUPAC Nomenclature Guide

Step 1: Identify the Parent Chain

Find the longest continuous carbon chain. The number of carbons dictates the root name:

  • 1 = meth-, 2 = eth-, 3 = prop-, 4 = but-, 5 = pent-
  • 6 = hex-, 7 = hept-, 8 = oct-, 9 = non-, 10 = dec-

Step 2: Identify the Functional Group (Suffix)

The highest priority group determines the ending of the name:

  • Alkanes: Single bonds only. Suffix: -ane
  • Alkenes: Carbon-carbon double bond. Suffix: -ene
  • Alkynes: Carbon-carbon triple bond. Suffix: -yne
  • Alcohols: Contains -OH. Suffix: -ol
  • Aldehydes: Contains -CHO at the end. Suffix: -al
  • Ketones: Contains C=O in the middle. Suffix: -one
  • Carboxylic Acids: Contains -COOH at the end. Suffix: -oic acid

Step 3: Numbering & Substituents (Prefixes)

Number the carbon chain starting from the end that gives the primary functional group the lowest number. Assign numbers to secondary substituents (like chloro, methyl) and list them alphabetically at the start.

  • Multiple Identical Substituents: If the exact same substituent appears more than once, use a Greek prefix (di- for 2, tri- for 3, tetra- for 4).
  • Punctuation Rules: Separate numbers from each other using a comma (e.g., 2,3) and separate numbers from letters using a hyphen (e.g., 2-chloro or 2,2-dimethyl).

Worked Examples

Review the logic below to understand how to correctly identify and build IUPAC names step-by-step.

CH3-CH(OH)-CH3

Example 1: Simple Molecule

  • Step 1A (Parent Chain): Count the longest continuous carbon chain. There are 3 carbons. The parent prefix is prop.
  • Step 1B (Functional Group): Look for the highest priority group. There is an -OH group. This makes it an alcohol.
  • Step 1C (Substituents): Are there any halogens or extra branches attached? No. Select none.
  • Step 2 (Building the Name): Number the chain to give the -OH group the lowest number (carbon 2).
    Assemble: prop + an + - + 2 + - + ol → propan-2-ol.
CH3-CO-CH2-CH(Cl)-CH2-CH2-CH3

Example 2: Complex Molecule

  • Step 1A (Parent Chain): The longest continuous carbon chain has 7 carbons. The parent prefix is hept.
  • Step 1B (Functional Group): The molecule contains a carbonyl group (C=O) in the middle of the chain. This makes it a ketone.
  • Step 1C (Substituents): There is a chlorine atom attached. The substituent is chloro.
  • Step 2 (Building the Name): Number from left to right to put the ketone on carbon 2 and the chlorine on carbon 4.
    Assemble: 4 + - + chloro + hept + an + - + 2 + - + one → 4-chloroheptan-2-one.
CH3-CH(Cl)-CH(Cl)-CH3

Example 3: Multiple Identical Substituents

  • Step 1A (Parent Chain): 4 carbons in a row. The parent prefix is but.
  • Step 1B (Functional Group): Only single bonds between carbons. It is an alkane.
  • Step 1C (Substituents): You see chlorine attached. Identify the substituent as simply chloro.
  • Step 2 (Building the Name): There are TWO chlorines, located on carbons 2 and 3. We must combine the numbers with a comma and use the prefix "di".
    Assemble: 2 + , + 3 + - + di + chloro + but + an + e → 2,3-dichlorobutane.
